Step 1 of 5
The problem
Example 10 · Tier 3P1 IncidentDual audienceTime pressure

It is 11:47am on a Tuesday. The Meridian Cloud Migration has been live in staging for three weeks. Arjun messages Sarah: the trading platform — one of the three zero-downtime applications — has been unavailable for 47 minutes. Mark Donovan has been notified and will call at 2pm.

Sarah has two hours to produce two completely different documents simultaneously:

Document 1 — Engineering RCA: Technical root cause analysis for Arjun and SF leadership — what happened, why, what failed in the process, immediate remediation steps, and what changes to prevent recurrence.
Document 2 — Board communication: A non-technical incident summary for Mark Donovan — business impact, what SF did, current status, and what Meridian can expect. Zero jargon. Confident and factual, not defensive.
Incident data — Arjun's raw log (11:51am)
Trading platform (trade-api) down since 11:00am. Alert triggered 11:03am — 3-min monitoring lag. Root cause: EBS volume IOPS throttling after migration to gp2 — should have been gp3 (provisioned IOPS). trade-api requires consistent 6,000 IOPS. gp2 at 500GB = 1,500 IOPS baseline, burst to 3,000 — insufficient under load. Volume migrated from on-prem SAN with dedicated 8,000 IOPS — not captured in infrastructure discovery doc. Failover did not trigger — auto-scaling group health check misconfigured (checked HTTP 200 on /status endpoint which returns 200 even when DB connection pool is exhausted). Fix: volume type migration to gp3, 6,000 IOPS provisioned. ETA 35 minutes. Rollback: restore from 6am snapshot — 5 hours data loss, not acceptable. Service restored: 12:24pm. Total downtime: 84 minutes.
Why this is Tier 3: The PM is under real time pressure managing two completely different stakeholder tracks from a single raw technical log. The naive approach — forwarding Arjun's log to Mark — would be a career-limiting move. The PM must triage, translate, and structure under pressure without losing accuracy.
2
Step 2 of 5
The naive approach

Under pressure, a PM might write one document and adapt it slightly for both audiences — or worse, forward Arjun's log with a covering note.

Sarah (naive — email to Mark)
Hi Mark — wanted to give you a quick update ahead of our 2pm call. We had an incident this morning with the trading platform. The EBS volume was provisioned as gp2 instead of gp3, causing IOPS throttling. The auto-scaling health check was also misconfigured. We've resolved it — total downtime was 84 minutes. Happy to walk through the details at 2pm.
What's wrong: "EBS volume," "gp2," "gp3," "IOPS throttling," and "auto-scaling health check" mean nothing to a CTO from a financial services background. The email reads as technical excuse-making. Mark will arrive at the 2pm call confused, anxious, and without the context to brief his board. "Happy to walk through the details" signals that Sarah hasn't prepared a proper response — she's deferring to the call.
The second failure: The engineering RCA was never written. Without it, the 2pm call will be a stream-of-consciousness explanation rather than a structured account. Every minute spent explaining instead of presenting is a credibility loss.
3
Step 3 of 5
The Claude interaction

Sarah works in two turns — engineering RCA first, board communication second. She provides the raw log and asks Claude to produce each document with explicit audience and format instructions.

Sarah — Turn 1 (Engineering RCA)
I'm a senior PM. We've had a P1 incident on a cloud migration project. I need a structured engineering RCA from the raw incident log below. Format: — Incident summary (2 sentences) — Timeline (key events with times) — Root cause (technical, specific — no softening) — Contributing factors — Immediate remediation steps taken — Process failures (what in our process allowed this to happen) — Corrective actions (specific, with owners and deadlines) Raw log: [pastes Arjun's log]
Claude — Turn 1
Engineering RCA — Trade-API Outage — May 13, 2026 Severity: P1 | Duration: 84 minutes | Status: Resolved Incident summary The Meridian trading platform (trade-api) experienced an 84-minute outage due to EBS volume IOPS throttling caused by incorrect volume type selection during migration. A misconfigured auto-scaling health check prevented automatic failover, extending the outage duration. Timeline 11:00am — trade-api becomes unavailable (IOPS exhaustion) 11:03am — Monitoring alert triggered (3-minute lag) 11:47am — PM notified, incident response initiated 12:24pm — Service restored (gp3 volume migration complete) Root cause The trade-api EBS volume was provisioned as gp2 (general purpose SSD) during migration. The on-premise SAN provided 8,000 dedicated IOPS; gp2 at 500GB provides only 1,500 baseline IOPS with burst to 3,000 — insufficient under production load. This misconfiguration was not caught during migration because the IOPS requirement was not documented in the infrastructure discovery artefact. Contributing factors — Infrastructure discovery document did not capture IOPS requirements for migrated volumes — Auto-scaling health check validated HTTP 200 on /status endpoint, which returns 200 even when the DB connection pool is exhausted — masking the actual service failure and preventing automatic failover Immediate remediation — EBS volume migrated from gp2 to gp3 with 6,000 IOPS provisioned — Auto-scaling health check reconfigured to validate DB connection pool health — Service restored 12:24pm Process failures 1. Infrastructure discovery did not include performance requirements (IOPS, throughput) as a mandatory field 2. Pre-migration validation checklist did not include a production-load IOPS validation step 3. Health check configuration was not reviewed against actual service failure modes Corrective actions — Update infrastructure discovery template to include IOPS/throughput requirements — Owner: Arjun — Due: May 20 — Audit all 14 migrated workloads for correct EBS volume type — Owner: Arjun — Due: May 17 — Update health check configurations for all migrated services — Owner: Arjun — Due: May 17 — Add IOPS validation to pre-migration checklist — Owner: Arjun — Due: May 20 — Review monitoring alert lag (3-minute delay is too long for P1 services) — Owner: Arjun — Due: May 20
Sarah — Turn 2 (Board communication)
Now write the board-level incident communication for Mark Donovan (Meridian CTO). He will share this with his board. Requirements: — No technical jargon (no EBS, gp2, gp3, IOPS, auto-scaling) — Lead with current status (resolved) — Plain-language explanation of what happened and why — What SF did to fix it — What we are doing to prevent recurrence — Confident and factual — not defensive, not over-apologetic — Maximum 200 words
Claude — Turn 2
Subject: Trading Platform Incident — Resolved | SF Update Mark — The trading platform was unavailable for 84 minutes this morning (11:00am–12:24pm). It is fully restored and operating normally. What happened: During the cloud migration, one component of the trading platform's storage configuration was not matched to the performance specifications of the original system. Under normal operating load, this caused the service to become unresponsive. An automated failover mechanism that should have activated did not do so because of a misconfiguration in the monitoring setup. What we did: Our engineering team identified and resolved the root cause within the incident window. The storage configuration has been corrected and the monitoring has been updated. What we are doing to prevent recurrence: We are auditing all migrated services this week to confirm performance configurations match original specifications. We are also updating our migration process to include this as a mandatory validation step before any future service goes live. I will send a full written RCA by end of day and will walk through it with you and Lisa on our standing call tomorrow. Sarah Chen | SF Senior PM
What changed: Two documents, two turns, 25 minutes. The engineering RCA is structured and actionable with named owners and deadlines. The board communication is 178 words, jargon-free, and leads with resolution — not explanation. Mark reads it and knows the situation is under control before the 2pm call.

Step 4 of 5
The refined output

Sarah makes two additions before sending. To the engineering RCA she adds a lessons-learned note that connects this incident to the risk register from project kickoff — Risk 1 (legacy infrastructure documentation gaps) was rated Red at the start of the project. To the board communication she adds the financial impact assessment she gets from Arjun.

✓ Addition to engineering RCA
Risk register cross-reference: This incident maps directly to Risk 1 in the project risk register (Legacy infrastructure documentation gaps — Likelihood 4, Impact 4, Score 16, RAG Red). The corrective actions above address the root cause of this risk. The risk register will be updated to reflect the remediation actions taken and the residual risk reassessed at the next project review.
✓ Addition to board communication
Business impact: The trading platform was unavailable for 84 minutes. Based on information from Meridian's operations team, no transactions were lost — the platform queues requests during downtime and processed them on restoration. We are confirming with Meridian IT that no data integrity issues resulted from the outage.
The PM's final layer: The risk register cross-reference turns the RCA from an incident report into a project management artefact — it shows Raj Patel that Sarah is connecting operational events to the project risk framework. The business impact addition gives Mark what his board will actually ask about: were any transactions lost? Claude could not have provided either — one required Sarah's knowledge of the risk register, the other required a conversation with Arjun.
5
Step 5 of 5
Watch out
1. Never send the engineering RCA to the client
The RCA contains phrases like "process failures" and "health check misconfigured" that will be read as admissions of negligence by a client's legal team. The board communication is the client-facing document. The RCA is internal. Keep them strictly separate.
2. Claude will be diplomatic about process failures
Without explicit instruction, Claude frames failures as "areas for improvement." In an RCA, this is insufficient — process failures need to be named plainly. The prompt explicitly asked for "no softening" on root cause and process failures. Always include this instruction.
3. Corrective action owners must be confirmed
Claude assigned all corrective actions to Arjun — which is correct in this case, but Claude doesn't know your team. Always confirm that the named owners have actually accepted the actions before the RCA is circulated.
The discipline that makes this work:
Produce the engineering RCA first — it forces you to understand the incident precisely. The board communication is then a translation exercise, not a creative one. Two turns, two documents, two audiences served. Never produce a single document for both.
