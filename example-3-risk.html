Step 1 of 5
The problem
Example 3 · Tier 1 Risk identification Single-context 2–3 turn interaction

The Meridian Cloud Migration project is entering its planning phase. Sarah needs to produce a risk register for the project kick-off — a document that will be reviewed by Raj Patel (SF Delivery Head) and shared with Lisa Park (Meridian PMO Lead).

The input she has is the project brief plus her knowledge of the Meridian context — a financial services firm with strict compliance requirements, an on-premise legacy infrastructure being migrated to AWS, and an aggressive Q4 deadline.

Project brief — Meridian Cloud Migration
Migrate Meridian's on-premise data centre workloads to AWS. Scope includes 14 application workloads, database migration (Oracle to Aurora PostgreSQL), network configuration (VPC, Direct Connect), and security/compliance setup (SOC 2, PCI DSS). Timeline: 9 months. Team: 6 SF engineers (cloud), 2 Meridian IT liaisons. Key constraint: zero downtime on 3 business-critical applications (trading platform, client reporting, risk engine). Legacy infrastructure is partially documented.

Risk registers are one of those deliverables that PMs often produce under time pressure and tend to be either too generic ("integration risk — medium") or too exhaustive to be useful. The challenge is producing a register that is specific, scored, and actionable — with mitigations that reflect the actual project context.

What makes cloud migration risks different: Unlike a greenfield build, migration projects have dependency risks on the legacy system state, compliance risks that are binary (you pass the audit or you don't), and zero-downtime requirements that make sequencing critical. A generic risk register won't capture these.

Step 2 of 5
The naive approach

The naive approach produces a risk register that looks complete but is generic enough to apply to any project:

Sarah (naive prompt)
Create a risk register for a cloud migration project.
Naive output — generic risk register (excerpt)
1. Timeline risk — Likelihood: Medium | Impact: High | Mitigation: Monitor progress weekly
2. Budget overrun — Likelihood: Medium | Impact: High | Mitigation: Track costs monthly
3. Technical complexity — Likelihood: Medium | Impact: Medium | Mitigation: Engage experienced engineers
4. Stakeholder misalignment — Likelihood: Low | Impact: Medium | Mitigation: Regular communication
5. Data loss during migration — Likelihood: Low | Impact: High | Mitigation: Back up data before migration
What's wrong: Every item is generic. "Monitor progress weekly" is not a mitigation — it's a standard PM practice. "Engage experienced engineers" tells you nothing about the actual risk. None of the Meridian-specific risks appear: the partially-documented legacy infrastructure, the zero-downtime requirement on three business-critical applications, the Oracle-to-Aurora database migration complexity, or the SOC 2 + PCI DSS compliance dependency. This register provides no value to Raj or Lisa.

Step 3 of 5
The Claude interaction

Sarah provides the specific project context, a scoring rubric, and instructions to surface Meridian-specific risks — not generic cloud migration risks.

Sarah — Turn 1
I'm a senior PM at a cloud consultancy. I need a risk register for the project described below. Use this scoring approach: Likelihood: 1 (unlikely) to 5 (almost certain) Impact: 1 (negligible) to 5 (project-ending) Risk score: Likelihood × Impact (1–25) RAG: Red (15–25), Amber (8–14), Green (1–7) Requirements: — Surface risks that are SPECIFIC to this project context, not generic cloud migration risks — Each risk must have a specific mitigation (not "monitor closely" or "communicate regularly") — Flag any risks that require CLIENT action to mitigate (not just SF action) — Include a "risk owner" for each item Project brief: [pastes brief]
Claude — Turn 1
Here is the Meridian Cloud Migration risk register: Risk 1: Legacy infrastructure documentation gaps Likelihood: 4 | Impact: 4 | Score: 16 | RAG: RED The brief notes legacy infrastructure is "partially documented." Undocumented dependencies or configurations will cause unplanned migration work. Mitigation: Run a 3-week discovery sprint before migration begins. Produce a dependency map for all 14 workloads. Do not begin migration until documentation is complete. Owner: Arjun (SF Cloud lead) + Meridian IT liaisons Client action required: YES — Meridian IT must provide access to all legacy system configurations and assign liaisons with sufficient knowledge to fill gaps. Risk 2: Zero-downtime requirement on 3 business-critical applications Likelihood: 3 | Impact: 5 | Score: 15 | RAG: RED Trading platform, client reporting, and risk engine cannot tolerate downtime. Any migration sequencing error or cutover failure creates immediate business and regulatory impact. Mitigation: Design blue-green deployment for all three applications. Mandate load testing in staging at 2× production load before cutover. Define rollback procedure with a maximum 15-minute RTO before cutover is approved. Owner: Arjun (SF Cloud lead) Client action required: YES — Meridian must define acceptable maintenance windows and confirm regulatory notification requirements for cutover events. Risk 3: Oracle to Aurora PostgreSQL migration complexity Likelihood: 4 | Impact: 4 | Score: 16 | RAG: RED Oracle-to-PostgreSQL schema and stored procedure incompatibilities are common and frequently underestimated. Financial services databases often contain complex procedural logic. Mitigation: Engage AWS Schema Conversion Tool in Week 1. Catalogue all stored procedures and triggers. Allocate a dedicated database engineer for conversion. Build a 4-week buffer for database migration in the project plan. Owner: Arjun (SF Cloud lead) Client action required: YES — Meridian DBA team must be available for knowledge transfer sessions. Risk 4: PCI DSS compliance scope expansion Likelihood: 3 | Impact: 4 | Score: 12 | RAG: AMBER PCI DSS scope may expand if the migrated architecture introduces new cardholder data flows not present in the legacy setup. This would require additional security controls and audit time. Mitigation: Engage a PCI DSS QSA in the architecture review phase (not post-migration). Document cardholder data flows explicitly in the architecture design document. Owner: Sarah Chen (PM) — escalate to Raj Patel if scope confirmed to expand Client action required: YES — Meridian must provide their current PCI DSS scoping document before architecture design begins. Risk 5: Direct Connect provisioning lead time Likelihood: 4 | Impact: 3 | Score: 12 | RAG: AMBER AWS Direct Connect provisioning typically takes 4–8 weeks. If not ordered at project start, it becomes a critical path blocker for the network configuration phase. Mitigation: Initiate Direct Connect order in Week 1. Do not schedule network configuration phase until provisioning is confirmed. Owner: Arjun (SF Cloud lead) Client action required: YES — Meridian IT must raise the procurement order and provide AWS account access immediately. Risk 6: Meridian IT liaison availability Likelihood: 3 | Impact: 3 | Score: 9 | RAG: AMBER The project relies on 2 Meridian IT liaisons. If these resources are pulled for BAU work, migration progress will stall on dependency resolution and testing sign-off. Mitigation: Formalise liaison commitment in the project charter — minimum 60% allocation for duration. Escalate to Lisa Park if availability drops below 40% in any given week. Owner: Sarah Chen (PM) Client action required: YES — Meridian IT management must confirm liaison availability in writing before project start.
What changed: Every risk is specific to Meridian's context. Every mitigation is actionable. Client-required actions are explicitly flagged — which turns the risk register into a client accountability document, not just an internal planning tool.

Step 5 of 5
Watch out
1. Claude will not surface industry-specific risks it wasn't told about
Claude doesn't know your client's regulatory environment, change freeze windows, or internal politics unless you tell it. Always add your own domain knowledge after the Claude output — treat the register as a first draft, not a finished product.
2. Scores are only as good as the context
Claude will score risks based on what's in the brief. If the brief undersells a risk (e.g. describes a complex legacy system as "partially documented"), Claude's likelihood score will be conservative. Validate every score against your own judgment before presenting.
3. Mitigations that require client action are easy to miss
Claude will produce SF-side mitigations by default. Explicitly ask it to flag client-required actions — otherwise these disappear into the mitigation column and never get raised at the kickoff.
The prompting discipline that makes this work:
(1) Provide specific project context — not a generic brief. (2) Give a scoring rubric — Claude will use it. (3) Ask explicitly for client-required actions. (4) Add your own domain knowledge after reviewing the output. A risk register is a living document — update it at each sprint review.
