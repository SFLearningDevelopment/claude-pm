Step 1 of 5
The problem
Example 5 · Tier 2 Project planning Multi-context 5–7 turn interaction

The Meridian Portal v1.0 scope document has been signed off. Sarah now needs to produce a milestone plan — the high-level delivery roadmap that shows the client when things will be built, what the dependencies are, and what the critical path looks like through to the September 30 go-live.

She has three inputs to work with simultaneously:

Input 1 — Confirmed scope (from Example 1 v1.0)
In scope: client account dashboard, report generation, document upload, user management, mobile-responsive frontend, Salesforce bi-directional sync, SOC 2 Type II compliance, full replacement of legacy Rails portal.
Hard go-live: September 30, 2026 | Budget: $380K | Team: Priya (web lead, 3 FE engineers), Rohan (backend lead, 1 BE engineer), QA shared
Input 2 — Known constraints and dependencies
— Salesforce integration requires Meridian IT to provision sandbox (estimated: 2 weeks from contract sign)
— SOC 2 audit requires minimum 30 days of system operation before audit can begin
— Brand guide from client: due Week 2, required before UI development can begin
— Legacy Rails portal: must remain live until new portal is fully validated in UAT
— UAT: Meridian requires 3 weeks minimum for client-side testing
— Contract signed: April 7, 2026
Input 3 — Team velocity baseline
Based on Sprint 1 and 2 data: ~34 story points per sprint (2-week sprints). Frontend is the constraint — Priya's team at 80% due to one part-time engineer.

This is a Tier 2 task because Sarah needs to reason across three inputs simultaneously — scope, constraints, and team capacity — to produce a plan that is both realistic and defensible to the client.

What makes this hard: The September 30 deadline is fixed. The SOC 2 requirement adds a hidden constraint — the system must be live in a staging-like environment by September 1 at the latest to allow 30 days of operation before the audit. The Salesforce dependency and UAT window further compress the available build time. A naive plan will miss these compounding constraints.

Step 2 of 5
The naive approach

The naive approach passes only the scope to Claude and asks for a plan — ignoring the constraints and capacity inputs:

Sarah (naive prompt)
Create a project plan for the Meridian Portal. Go-live is September 30. The scope includes account dashboard, report generation, document upload, user management, mobile frontend, Salesforce integration, and SOC 2 compliance.
Naive output — generic phase plan
Phase 1 — Discovery (Weeks 1–2): Requirements gathering, technical architecture
Phase 2 — Development (Weeks 3–18): Build all features
Phase 3 — Testing (Weeks 19–22): QA and UAT
Phase 4 — Go-live (Week 23): Deploy and launch

Milestones: Design complete Week 4 · Development complete Week 18 · UAT complete Week 22 · Go-live Week 23
What's wrong: This plan is structurally useless. "Development (Weeks 3–18)" hides 16 weeks of undifferentiated build work — the client has no visibility into what gets built when. The SOC 2 constraint is completely missing. There is no Salesforce integration dependency. UAT is 3 weeks but the Meridian requirement is also 3 weeks — so the plan has zero buffer. And "Phase 2 — Build all features" assumes parallel development that the team capacity doesn't support.

Step 3 of 5
The Claude interaction

Sarah provides all three inputs and asks Claude to reason through the constraints before producing a plan.

Sarah — Turn 1
I'm a senior PM at a cloud consultancy. Before you produce a project plan, analyse the constraints below and tell me: 1. What is the latest possible start date for each phase, working backwards from September 30? 2. Which constraint creates the most compression on the build window? 3. Are there any constraints that conflict with the September 30 deadline? Contract start: April 7, 2026 Hard go-live: September 30, 2026 Constraints: — SOC 2 audit requires 30 days of system operation in a production-equivalent environment before audit — UAT: 3 weeks minimum, Meridian-side — Salesforce sandbox: 2 weeks to provision from contract sign (available ~April 21) — Brand guide required before UI development begins (due April 14, now April 19 — 5 days late) — Legacy portal must remain live until UAT complete — Team velocity: ~34 story points per 2-week sprint — Frontend is the bottleneck (Priya at 80% capacity)
Claude — Turn 1
Backwards scheduling analysis — September 30 go-live Working back from September 30: Latest possible dates (working backwards): — Go-live: September 30 — UAT complete: September 30 → UAT start: September 9 (3 weeks) — SOC 2: requires 30 days operation → staging must be live by September 1 — Staging deploy: September 1 → all development must be complete by August 29 — Development window: April 21 (Salesforce available) to August 29 = 18.5 weeks = ~9 sprints — UI development start: blocked until brand guide received (currently April 19) Most compressive constraint: SOC 2. The 30-day operation requirement means the system must be in a production-equivalent environment by September 1 — not September 30. This compresses the available build window by 4 weeks that most plans would incorrectly treat as buffer. Conflicts with September 30: One potential conflict: if UAT starts September 9 and the SOC 2 clock starts September 1, these run in parallel. This is feasible IF staging is fully deployed by September 1, but there is zero buffer for a delayed staging deploy. Any slip in development completion beyond August 29 pushes both UAT and the SOC 2 clock — and risks the audit deadline, not just the go-live date. The real constraint: The effective end of build is August 29. That gives 18.5 weeks of development from April 21, with a frontend bottleneck. This is tight but achievable if Salesforce integration and compliance work are sequenced correctly.
Sarah — Turn 2
Good. Now build the milestone plan. Use these phases: — Discovery and architecture (can begin April 7, before Salesforce sandbox) — Core portal build (authentication, dashboard, report generation) — Document upload + user management — Salesforce integration (cannot start before April 21) — SOC 2 compliance hardening — UAT (Meridian-side, 3 weeks) — Go-live For each phase: start date, end date, key deliverables, dependencies, and risk flags. Mark the critical path.
Claude — Turn 2
Meridian Portal — Milestone Plan v0.1 Start: April 7, 2026 | Go-live: September 30, 2026 Phase 1 — Discovery and architecture April 7 – April 25 (3 weeks) Deliverables: Technical architecture document, data model, API contract, deployment architecture Dependencies: None (can begin immediately) ⚠ Risk: Brand guide delay (5 days) compresses UI design time — flag to client Phase 2 — Core portal build April 28 – June 6 (6 weeks / 3 sprints) Deliverables: Authentication, client dashboard, report generation Dependencies: Architecture complete, brand guide received ⚠ Risk: Frontend bottleneck — Priya at 80% capacity. Dashboard and report generation are frontend-heavy. Phase 3 — Document upload + user management June 9 – June 27 (3 weeks / 1.5 sprints) Deliverables: Document upload module, admin user management interface Dependencies: Core portal complete (authentication required) Phase 4 — Salesforce integration April 21 – July 11 (runs partially in parallel with Phase 2 and 3) Deliverables: Bi-directional Salesforce sync, data mapping, integration tests Dependencies: Salesforce sandbox live (April 21) ⚠ Risk: Salesforce integration scope is undefined (data entities, sync frequency). Must be confirmed in Phase 1. Phase 5 — SOC 2 compliance hardening July 14 – August 29 (7 weeks) Deliverables: Security controls implementation, logging, access management, evidence collection Dependencies: All features complete (cannot harden a moving target) ⚠ CRITICAL PATH: Compliance hardening must complete by August 29. System must be in production-equivalent environment by September 1 (30-day SOC 2 clock). Phase 6 — UAT (Meridian-side) September 9 – September 30 (3 weeks) Deliverables: Client sign-off on all portal functions, defect resolution Dependencies: SOC 2 staging complete, legacy portal remains live ⚠ Risk: Zero buffer. Any Phase 5 slip pushes UAT into the go-live date. Go-live: September 30, 2026 Critical path: Phase 1 → Phase 2 (brand guide dependency) → Phase 5 (SOC 2 clock) → Phase 6 (UAT) → Go-live
What changed: The plan is now constraint-aware. The SOC 2 compression is visible. The Salesforce integration runs in parallel — correctly. The critical path is explicitly identified. Sarah can present this to Raj Patel and Mark Donovan with confidence that it reflects reality, not aspiration.

Step 5 of 5
Watch out
1. Claude reasons forward, not backward
Left to its own devices, Claude will produce a forward-scheduled plan — starting from Day 1 and working to the deadline. For fixed-deadline projects, always ask Claude to reason backwards first. The backwards analysis surfaces compression that forward scheduling hides.
2. Compliance constraints are often underspecified
SOC 2 "30-day operation" is a real constraint — but what counts as "production-equivalent" varies by auditor. Claude will model what you tell it; it cannot know your specific auditor's interpretation. Always validate compliance timeline assumptions with your compliance lead before presenting to the client.
3. Parallel phases need explicit sequencing rules
Claude correctly ran the Salesforce integration in parallel with the build phases — but only because Sarah specified it. If you don't tell Claude what can run in parallel, it will default to sequential phases and produce an unrealistically long plan.
The prompting discipline that makes this work:
(1) Separate the constraint analysis from the plan generation — two turns, not one. (2) Always ask for backwards scheduling on fixed-deadline projects. (3) Specify which phases can run in parallel. (4) Add your own operational buffer — Claude will not add it unless asked. (5) The plan Claude produces is a first draft — validate every date with your tech leads before sharing with the client.
