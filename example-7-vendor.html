Example 7 · Tier 2 Project reporting Health narrative Multi-context

It is the end of Month 2 on the Meridian engagement. SF has a monthly project health reporting obligation — a structured report that goes to Raj Patel internally and to Lisa Park at Meridian. The report must cover all three workstreams: Cloud Migration, Meridian Portal, and Meridian AI Assistant.

Sarah has three separate status dumps from her workstream leads:

Workstream data — Cloud Migration (Arjun)
Status: On track overall. Legacy infrastructure discovery complete — found 2 undocumented applications not in original scope. Direct Connect ordered (Week 1). ETA provisioning: May 5. Oracle to Aurora conversion started — 3 of 14 workloads analysed. Zero-downtime plan drafted for trading platform. Budget: $62K of $180K (34% budget, 22% timeline — slightly ahead). Risk: undocumented apps need scope discussion with client.
Workstream data — Meridian Portal (Priya)
Status: Velocity declining (see sprint tracking). Authentication complete. Dashboard 80% done. Report generation not started — blocked on brand guide (10 days late now). Salesforce sandbox received but integration not started due to capacity. Budget: $47K of $380K (12%). Risk: velocity at 78% and declining. Frontend bottleneck. Brand guide still outstanding.
Workstream data — AI Assistant (Carlos)
Status: Discovery phase complete. RAG pipeline architecture agreed. Data access request submitted to Meridian IT — awaiting response (submitted 2 weeks ago, no response). Build cannot start without data access. Timeline at risk if not resolved this week. Budget: $8K of $120K (7% — behind plan due to blocked start).

The monthly report needs to synthesise these three dumps into a coherent executive narrative — not three separate summaries. The client wants to understand the overall programme health, the key risks across all workstreams, and what actions they need to take.

The synthesis challenge: Three workstreams each have their own issues — but the client reads this as one programme. Sarah needs to surface the cross-cutting themes (two of three workstreams have a client-side blocker), set an honest overall RAG status, and present a coherent picture without alarming the client unnecessarily or downplaying real risks.

The naive approach

The naive approach asks Claude to summarise the three workstreams without providing synthesis guidance:

Sarah (naive prompt)
Write a monthly project health report from these three workstream updates. [pastes three workstream dumps]
Naive output — three summaries stapled together
Monthly Project Health Report — Month 2

Cloud Migration: On track. Discovery complete with 2 undocumented apps found. Direct Connect ordered. Oracle conversion started. Budget on track at 34%.

Meridian Portal: Velocity declining at 78%. Authentication complete. Dashboard 80% done. Report generation blocked on brand guide. Salesforce integration not started.

AI Assistant: Discovery complete. RAG architecture agreed. Build blocked pending data access from Meridian IT. Timeline at risk.
What's wrong: This is not a health report — it's a copy-paste of the workstream leads' notes with headings added. There is no overall RAG status. No cross-cutting theme analysis. No client action section. The two client-side blockers (brand guide + data access) are buried separately in two workstream sections. A client reading this would not understand the programme's overall health or what they need to do.

Step 5 of 5
Watch out
1. Claude will produce three summaries, not one programme view
Without explicit synthesis instructions, Claude treats multi-workstream data as three separate reports. Always instruct Claude to identify cross-cutting themes — risks or blockers that appear in multiple workstreams are always more important than workstream-specific issues.
2. Client-side blockers will be softened by default
Claude tends to frame client inaction diplomatically. "Data access request submitted — awaiting response" reads as a process step, not a blocker. Explicitly instruct Claude to flag client-side delays as programme risks, not administrative items.
3. Overall RAG requires your judgment, not Claude's calculation
Claude will attempt to derive an overall RAG from the workstream statuses. But the overall RAG is a PM judgment call — it depends on what the client cares about most, what the contractual obligations are, and what the delivery head needs to know. Always review Claude's proposed overall RAG before publishing.
The prompting discipline that makes this work:
(1) Give Claude a report structure — don't let it invent one. (2) Instruct it to find cross-cutting themes before writing. (3) Ask for a consolidated client action section explicitly. (4) Ask for "what went well" separately — Claude defaults to risk-focused output. (5) Add the visual presentation layer yourself — Claude's text output won't match how your client actually uses the report.
